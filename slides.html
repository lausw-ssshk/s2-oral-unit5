How to open strong · How to close clearly

Openings

Why a strong opening matters

  • Get people interested — listeners decide quickly whether to pay attention.
  • Interact with the audience — a question or situation pulls them in.
  • Then signal your plan: There are three reasons why…
Openings · A–D

Four opening styles

Same topic: Japan as a tourist destination. Which feels strongest?

A · Plain
Today I want to talk about Japan.
B · Clear + friendly
Hello everyone. Today I’d like to talk about my favourite tourist destination, Japan.
C · Asking a question
Good morning everyone. Do you like travelling? Me too. So today I’d like to talk about my favourite tourist destination, Japan.
D · Creating a situation
Hi everyone. Suppose you could fly to any country right now — where would you go? Today I’d like to talk about my favourite tourist destination, Japan.

C and D usually work better — they involve the audience.

Closings

Ending in 3 steps

1

Give an overall idea

In conclusion… / In short… / Overall, I think…

2

Say the presentation is over

I hope my presentation was interesting. Thank you. · That’s all I want to say. Thank you. · That’s the end of my presentation. Thank you.

3

Optional: challenge or encourage

Why don’t you start and try it out today? · How are we going to change the world? · What will happen if you don’t act now?

Language bank

Useful phrases

Openings

  • Hello / Good morning, everyone.
  • Today I’d like to talk about…
  • Do you like…? / Have you ever…?
  • Suppose you could… / Imagine if…
  • There are three reasons why…

Closings

  • In conclusion… / In short… / Overall, I think…
  • That’s all I want to say. / That’s the end of my presentation.
  • I hope my presentation was interesting. Thank you.
  • Why don’t you…? / What will happen if…?
Try it

Mini practice — a book you like

Task: Prepare a short beginning and ending for a talk about a book you like.

  • Opening: Use style B, C, or D (friendly · question · situation).
  • Ending: Overall idea → say it’s over → optional challenge/question.
  • Keep it short — about 30–45 seconds when spoken.

Example start: “Have you ever finished a book and still thought about it days later? Today I’d like to talk about…”

Remember

Quick recap

  • Open to interest and involve — questions and situations beat a flat start.
  • Close in three steps: overall idea → it’s over → optional challenge.
  • Practise with a real topic (e.g. a book) so the phrases feel natural.
